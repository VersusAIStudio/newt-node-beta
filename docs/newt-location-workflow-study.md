## Central Lesson

Design and select the location for the action it must support, not merely for its standalone beauty. Steven imagines where the hikers will stand and move, where the avalanche will originate, and how the terrain makes that sequence readable. The best-looking landscape is not automatically the best production reference.

## Observed Workflow

### 1. Start From A Suitable Preset

At 00:00-01:05, character preparation already exists in a separate project. Steven makes and saves a location project, opens a clean canvas, and inserts the Cinematic Location preset through Newt. He removes the Newt node after using its preset picker.

The reusable lesson is to inspect and reuse an appropriate saved workflow before building from scratch. The separate project and removal of Newt are manual workspace choices, not instructions for an agent to switch away from an active project or delete itself.

### 2. Describe Geography In Terms Of Future Action

At 01:05-02:33, the subject is a steep, snow-covered Arctic mountain slope rising diagonally through the composition. Mist establishes a cold, hazy environment. The terrain is intentionally difficult because hikers will later traverse it, encounter an avalanche and escape.

The reference is not asked to perform the entire sequence. Geography and atmosphere prepare for future staging; the people, blizzard motion and avalanche action come later. Treat the mentioned historical period as scene intent, not independently verified historical research.

### 3. Keep The Plate Clean And The Controls Distinct

At 02:33-04:31, the preset supplies a three-quarter-view direction and excludes people and animals. Steven retains WS and 35mm with Camera Type set to None. He changes Style to Vintage 8mm and Grade to Jaws of Life to suit this older-looking, cold scene.

The frame at 03:56 shows Camera and Style connected to Image Model, with Nano Banana Pro, six generations, 21:9 and 2K. These are demonstrated settings, not universal defaults. Steven explicitly presents Cinematic Standard/Indie and a cool grade as possible alternatives.

Keep location geography, camera framing, photographic treatment and palette separate. An archival-looking treatment is an aesthetic choice; it is not evidence that the depicted historical period was photographed on 8mm film. Do not require a supplied mood board or character reference when this clean-plate task does not need one.

### 4. Curate By Look And Staging Potential

At 04:39-07:10, Steven compares six candidates, opens images at a larger size, and narrows the selection. Rejection reasons include an unwanted tint, excessive vignetting and too much retro treatment. Matching a requested style does not excuse overdoing it.

The two remaining candidates are visibly compared at 06:55. Brightness, cooler tones and overall cinematic feel matter, but the decisive question is whether the slope supports the hikers and avalanche. Steven favors the steeper option even though the alternative is also attractive.

An agent should record specific observed reasons, not merely say that one candidate is best. Candidate count and retries must remain bounded by the task's quality target, permissions and budget; this demonstration is not a rule to always buy six images.

### 5. Fix A Local Defect Without Regeneration

At 07:10-08:19, Steven retains the selected image in a separate Image node. He likes its texture and much of its vignette but dislikes the curved, beveled-looking border. In the image viewer, he makes a small crop while holding Shift to retain aspect ratio, then applies it.

The frame at 07:54 shows the crop inset from the outer border. This is a local, non-generative correction to a useful image, not a new request to an image model. Preserve the useful terrain and intended aspect; do not crop away the space needed for the planned action.

### 6. Retain A Clear Deliverable And Clean Up Selectively

At 08:09-09:21, Steven names the retained image Location, marks its node green, removes the no-longer-needed generation workflow from the canvas and saves. The frame at 08:45 shows the independent green Location node.

He explicitly says the creation workflow should be kept when more related environments or scenes may need it. Removal is appropriate here because the short scene needs just this one location. This does not authorize deleting media files, History, unrelated nodes, reusable presets or protected work. A green highlight is an organizational cue, not evidence of independent quality verification.

## Comparison With Current Skills

The current Workflow Planning skill already prefers suitable saved presets. Cinematic Narrative already calls for spatial depth, action-relevant features and clean location plates. Asset Selection and Review already requires observed candidate comparisons, bounded retries and supported cropping. Canvas Organization already separates durable workflows from temporary preparation and guards retention before canvas-only cleanup.

This tutorial adds useful specificity rather than requiring a duplicate instruction system:

1. **Action-based location review:** assess intended performer positions, a traversable route, the originating event or hazard, and whether the composition supports that sequence. Apply these criteria only when relevant to the brief.
2. **Style restraint:** reject distracting tint, borders or excessive treatment even when the named style was requested. Judge the intended look, not keyword compliance.
3. **Local finishing before paid retry:** consider a small aspect-preserving crop when the only defect is at the edge and essential staging remains intact. Never claim the edit unless an available tool actually performs it.
4. **Explicit handoff:** keep the exact selected or edited full-resolution image in an independent, clearly named asset node. Retain the source workflow whenever revision or reuse remains plausible.

## Proposed Next Implementation

Refine the existing Cinematic Narrative and Asset Selection skills with concise, applicable rules rather than loading this entire transcript or making every task follow an Arctic/vintage recipe. Bump the changed system skill versions and preserve users' Custom overrides. New tasks should capture the revised versions; already running tasks should not silently change their instructions.

Before promising automated finishing, verify that the agent's executable tools expose the required crop and output-retention operations. A feature available to a person in the thumbnail viewer is not automatically available to Newt. Keep unsupported actions explicit and do not simulate them by rewriting protected output fields.

Use a held-out evaluation: request a location for a different action, such as two people crossing a station toward an arriving train. Check that Newt starts from a relevant current preset, prepares a suitable empty plate, selects usable staging space and sightlines, avoids importing the mountain example's styling, and retains the chosen asset. Include candidates that are beautiful but spatially unsuitable, over-stylized, and usable with only a small edge crop.

Run deterministic ownership, capability and budget tests first. Any paid visual evaluation needs a separately authorized budget and an actual assessment of the resulting images. This study made no generation requests and made no runtime changes.
